About the course

An introduction to NeuroAI, the field that brings brain science and artificial intelligence together. Students build a mathematical and computational toolkit for studying brain, mind and behavior, from classical models to hands-on work with modern machine learning in Python.

The course has four parts: representational spaces, and how to read them out and compare them; learning representations, including vision and model–brain comparison; sequence models, from recurrent dynamics to attention and language; and generative models. Explainability runs through all four. For each model we ask what it computes, how it computes it, and whether the same account holds for the brain.

The course is for advanced undergraduates and graduate students in cognitive science, neuroscience, psychology, computer science and applied mathematics. It assumes some programming experience but no particular disciplinary background.
